How we work

Note / 01

“The design of the door should indicate how to work it without any need for signs, certainly without any need for trial and error.”

Don Norman · The Design of Everyday Things, 2013

What we take from it

If a page needs a line telling people where to click, we change the page, not the line. It's the same rule for a form, a basket or a menu on a small phone.

Note / 02

“Type well used is invisible as type.”

Beatrice Warde · The Crystal Goblet, a lecture in London, 1930

What we take from it

We pick type for the person reading on a bus, not for our portfolio. If they notice the font before the words, we've overdone it.

Note / 03

In 1943 pilots kept raising the wheels instead of the flaps after landing. The two levers sat side by side and looked the same. Alphonse Chapanis fixed a small rubber wheel to one and a wedge to the other, and the mistakes stopped.

Alphonse Chapanis · as told in Stanley Roscoe, The Adolescence of Engineering Psychology, 1997

What we take from it

When people keep making the same mistake on a site, the site is wrong. We look for the two buttons that look alike.

Note / 04

“Any artifact should stand by itself in all its clarity.”

Massimo Vignelli · The Vignelli Canon, 2010

What we take from it

Someone who lands on one page from a search should understand it without the home page to explain it. That's why every project here has its own page.

Note / 05

“Testing one user is 100 percent better than testing none.”

Steve Krug · Don't Make Me Think, Revisited, 2014

What we take from it

Watching one person use a page tells us more than an afternoon of arguing about it.

Note / 06

In 1999 NASA lost the Mars Climate Orbiter. One file gave thruster figures in imperial units where the rest of the system expected metric, and the reviews didn't catch it.

NASA Mishap Investigation Board · Phase I report, 1999

What we take from it

Things break where two parts meet: a form and the server, a price and the checkout. We put a check at each join.

Note / 07

“Vulgar ostentation is twice as easy as discipline.”

Beatrice Warde · The Crystal Goblet, a lecture in London, 1930

What we take from it

Adding things is quick. Most of our design time goes on deciding what to take out.

Note / 08

“Design depends largely on constraints.”

Charles Eames · Design Q&A, Eames Office, 1972

What we take from it

A small budget or a fixed launch date isn't in the way of the design. It's most of the brief.
