What we keep coming back to

A line from someone who built things before us, and what it means for a site we'd build for you. A new one each day.

How we work

Note / 01

“The design of the door should indicate how to work it without any need for signs, certainly without any need for trial and error.”

Don Norman · The Design of Everyday Things, 2013

What we take from it

If a page needs a line telling people where to click, we change the page, not the line. It's the same rule for a form, a basket or a menu on a small phone.

Note / 02

“Type well used is invisible as type.”

Beatrice Warde · The Crystal Goblet, a lecture in London, 1930

What we take from it

We pick type for the person reading on a bus, not for our portfolio. If they notice the font before the words, we've overdone it.

Note / 03

In 1943 pilots kept raising the wheels instead of the flaps after landing. The two controls sat side by side and looked almost the same. Alphonse Chapanis's fix was a small rubber wheel on one and a wedge on the other, and on the planes that got it, the mistakes stopped.

Alphonse Chapanis · as told in Stanley Roscoe, The Adolescence of Engineering Psychology, 1997

What we take from it

When people keep making the same mistake on a site, the site is wrong. We look for the two buttons that look alike.

Note / 04

“Any artifact should stand by itself in all its clarity.”

Massimo Vignelli · The Vignelli Canon, 2010

What we take from it

Someone who lands on one page from a search should understand it without the home page to explain it. That's why every project here has its own page.

Note / 05

“Testing one user is 100 percent better than testing none.”

Steve Krug · Don't Make Me Think, Revisited, 2014

What we take from it

Watching one person use a page tells us more than an afternoon of arguing about it.

Note / 06

In 1999 NASA lost the Mars Climate Orbiter. One file gave thruster figures in imperial units where the rest of the system expected metric, and the reviews didn't catch it.

NASA Mishap Investigation Board · Phase I report, 1999

What we take from it

Things break where two parts meet: a form and the server, a price and the checkout. We put a check at each join.

Note / 07

“Vulgar ostentation is twice as easy as discipline.”

Beatrice Warde · The Crystal Goblet, a lecture in London, 1930

What we take from it

Adding things is quick. Most of our design time goes on deciding what to take out.

Note / 08

“Design depends largely on constraints.”

Charles Eames · Design Q&A, Eames Office, 1972

What we take from it

A small budget or a fixed launch date isn't in the way of the design. It's most of the brief.

Note / 09

“Never use a long word where a short one will do.”

George Orwell · “Politics and the English Language”, 1946

What we take from it

“Buy” beats “Proceed to purchase”. We write button labels the way you'd say them out loud.

Note / 10

In 1996 the first Ariane 5 broke up 37 seconds after lift-off. Its guidance software came from Ariane 4, including a function that “serves no purpose” after lift-off and was kept running only because Ariane 4 needed it.

Ariane 5 Flight 501 Inquiry Board · report, Paris, 1996

What we take from it

A part that worked on one site can still break on the next. When we reuse a form or a plugin, we test it on the new site as if it were new.

Note / 11

“Have nothing in your houses that you do not know to be useful, or believe to be beautiful.”

William Morris · “The Beauty of Life”, a lecture, 1880

What we take from it

The same goes for a home page. Each section should help someone or be something they'd miss. The rest comes out.

Note / 12

“The first principle is that you must not fool yourself—and you are the easiest person to fool.”

Richard Feynman · “Cargo Cult Science”, Caltech, 1974

What we take from it

The easiest way for us to fool ourselves is to check a page on our own big screen. So before we call it done, we measure it on a phone-sized screen, 375 pixels wide.

Note / 13

On 1 August 2012 Knight Capital lost more than $460 million in about 45 minutes. New code had gone to seven of eight servers. On the eighth, old code unused for years woke up when a setting was reused, and nobody had checked the release.

US Securities and Exchange Commission · order on Knight Capital, 2013

What we take from it

Old features switched off are still there. When we remove something from a site, we remove all of it.

Note / 14

“Omit needless words.”

William Strunk Jr. · The Elements of Style, 1920

What we take from it

A page that says less gets read more. Every sentence on a site has to earn its place.
