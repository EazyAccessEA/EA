## Patterns
- They lead with a low price: £149, £199, £400.
- "Free website", with a monthly hosting charge that's the real price.
- Scarcity claims: "3 spaces left", "one week only".
- Review counts and stars.
- Almost all of them send people to a form. Only one click-to-WhatsApp ad turned up, and it isn't UK.

## Gaps we can truthfully fill
1. **You own the domain, hosting and logins.** The "free site, £20/month" offers keep them. Nobody says it outright.
2. **A stated reply time.** Nobody gives one. Ours is two working days.
3. **The person who replies builds it.** The nearest is Eccleshall's "no faceless agency".
4. **A fixed price per stage, nothing extra unless agreed in writing.**
5. **The launch date agreed before work starts.**

## Rules that apply
- **CAP 3.7:** keep evidence for every claim.
- **CAP 3.17–3.18:** prices include VAT unless the audience is clearly business-only.
- **CAP 3.22–3.23:** a "from £X" price must really be available.
- **CAP 3.31:** no false urgency.
- **CAP 3.3:** "fixed price per stage" needs to say what a stage is and that changes need written agreement.
- Web design isn't a Meta special ad category.

(The policy notes come from the researcher's own knowledge, not from fetched sources.)
