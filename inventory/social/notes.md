# Daily notes: the checked source list

Every note is checked against its primary source before it's scheduled. Each one records the page or URL where the wording was seen. A note without a reference doesn't post.

Notes 01–08 were checked on 20 Nov 2026. "Social wording" is the text the posts use. Where it differs from the site (`pages/index.html`), the reason is given.

## 01, Don Norman
- Quote: "The design of the door should indicate how to work it without any need for signs, certainly without any need for trial and error."
- Source: The Design of Everyday Things, Revised and Expanded Edition (Basic Books, 2013), ch. 1, p. 2.
- Takeaway: If a page needs a line telling people where to click, we change the page, not the line. It's the same rule for a form, a basket or a menu on a small phone.

## 02, Beatrice Warde
- Quote: "Type well used is invisible as type."
- Source: "The Crystal Goblet", a lecture to the British Typographers' Guild, London, October 1930. Reprinted in Armstrong (ed.), Graphic Design Theory (Princeton Architectural Press, 2009), p. 41.
- Social source line: Beatrice Warde · "The Crystal Goblet", a lecture in London, 1930. The quote marks show it's the lecture; the book of that name came out in 1955.
- Takeaway: We pick type for the person reading on a bus, not for our portfolio. If they notice the font before the words, we've overdone it.

## 03, Alphonse Chapanis
- Story (social wording): In 1943 pilots kept raising the wheels instead of the flaps after landing. The two controls sat side by side and looked almost the same. Chapanis's fix was a small rubber wheel on one and a wedge on the other, and on the planes that got it, the mistakes stopped.
- Why it differs from the site: Roscoe says "identical toggle switches or nearly identical levers", and that the shapes "were attached". It doesn't say Chapanis fitted them himself.
- Source: Stanley Roscoe, The Adolescence of Engineering Psychology (HFES, 1997), p. 2. https://www.hfes.org/Portals/0/Documents/adolescence.pdf
- Takeaway: When people keep making the same mistake on a site, the site is wrong. We look for the two buttons that look alike.

## 04, Massimo Vignelli
- Quote: "Any artifact should stand by itself in all its clarity."
- Source: The Vignelli Canon (Lars Müller, 2010), "Pragmatics", PDF p. 8. https://www.rit.edu/vignellicenter/sites/rit.edu.vignellicenter/files/documents/The%20Vignelli%20Canon.pdf
- Takeaway: Someone who lands on one page from a search should understand it without the home page to explain it. That's why every project here has its own page.

## 05, Steve Krug
- Quote: "Testing one user is 100 percent better than testing none."
- Source: Don't Make Me Think, Revisited (2014), ch. 9. Seen in the publisher's excerpt: https://www.peachpit.com/articles/article.aspx?p=2209313 (no page number seen).
- Takeaway: Watching one person use a page tells us more than an afternoon of arguing about it.

## 06, Mars Climate Orbiter
- Story (social wording): In 1999 NASA lost the Mars Climate Orbiter. One ground software file gave thruster figures in pound-seconds where the rest of the system expected newton-seconds, and the project's checks didn't catch it.
- Why it differs from the site: the report says "English units", and that the cause "was not caught by the processes in-place". The site's "imperial" and "reviews" are fair paraphrases. The social wording is closer to the report.
- Source: Mars Climate Orbiter Mishap Investigation Board, Phase I Report, 10 Nov 1999, p. 6 and p. 16. https://llis.nasa.gov/llis_lib/pdf/1009464main1_0641-mr.pdf
- Takeaway: Things break where two parts meet: a form and the server, a price and the checkout. We put a check at each join.

## 07, Beatrice Warde
- Quote: "Vulgar ostentation is twice as easy as discipline."
- Source: as 02, p. 43.
- Takeaway: Adding things is quick. Most of our design time goes on deciding what to take out.

## 08, Charles Eames
- Quote: "Design depends largely on constraints." (The answer to "Does the creation of Design admit constraint?")
- Source: Design Q&A, a film by Charles and Ray Eames, 1972. The answers were first written in 1969 for the Paris exhibition "Qu'est-ce que le design?". Text: https://www.hermanmiller.com/stories/why-magazine/design-q-and-a-charles-and-ray-eames/ and https://www.eamesoffice.com/the-work/design-q-a/
- Social source line: Charles Eames · Design Q&A, 1972. The Eames Office distributes the film; it isn't the publisher, so it's left off.
- Takeaway: A small budget or a fixed launch date isn't in the way of the design. It's most of the brief.

## 09, George Orwell
- Quote: "Never use a long word where a short one will do."
- Source: "Politics and the English Language", 1946, rule ii. https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/
- Takeaway: "Buy" beats "Proceed to purchase". We write button labels the way you'd say them out loud.

## 10, Ariane 5
- Story: In 1996 the first Ariane 5 broke up 37 seconds after lift-off. Its guidance software came from Ariane 4, including a function that "serves no purpose" after lift-off and was kept running only because Ariane 4 needed it.
- Source: Ariane 5 Flight 501 Failure, Report by the Inquiry Board, Paris, 19 July 1996. https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~krj/courses/2307/disasters/ariane5rep.html
- Takeaway: A part that worked on one site can still break on the next. When we reuse a form or a plugin, we test it on the new site as if it were new.

## 11, William Morris
- Quote: "Have nothing in your houses that you do not know to be useful, or believe to be beautiful."
- Source: "The Beauty of Life", lecture, 1880, in Hopes and Fears for Art (1882), p. 108.
- Takeaway: The same goes for a home page. Each section should help someone or be something they'd miss. The rest comes out.

## 12, Richard Feynman
- Quote: "The first principle is that you must not fool yourself—and you are the easiest person to fool."
- Source: "Cargo Cult Science", Caltech commencement address, 1974. https://calteches.library.caltech.edu/51/2/CargoCult.htm
- Takeaway: The easiest way for us to fool ourselves is to check a page on our own big screen. So before we call it done, we measure it on a phone-sized screen, 375 pixels wide.

## 13, Knight Capital
- Story: On 1 August 2012 Knight Capital lost more than $460 million in about 45 minutes. New code had gone to seven of eight servers. On the eighth, old code unused for years woke up when a setting was reused, and nobody had checked the release.
- Source: US SEC, Order, Release No. 34-70694 (2013), ¶¶13–15. https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/admin/2013/34-70694.pdf
- Takeaway: Old features switched off are still there. When we remove something from a site, we remove all of it.

## 14, William Strunk Jr.
- Quote: "Omit needless words."
- Source: The Elements of Style (1920), rule 13, p. 24.
- Takeaway: A page that says less gets read more. Every sentence on a site has to earn its place.

## 15, Tim Berners-Lee
- Quote: "Cool URIs don't change."
- Source: "Cool URIs don't change", W3C, 1998. https://www.w3.org/Provider/Style/URI
- Takeaway: When a site is rebuilt, old links to it should still work. Every old address gets sent to its new page, so someone who saved a link doesn't hit a dead end.

## 16, Paul Fitts
- Story: In 1954 Paul Fitts had people tap a stylus between two metal plates while he changed their size and the gap between them. The smaller and further apart the targets, the longer each tap took, in a pattern he could predict.
- Source: Journal of Experimental Psychology 47 (1954), 381–391. https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall08/cos436/FittsJEP1954.pdf
- Takeaway: Small buttons get missed. Every tap target on our sites is at least 24 by 24 pixels, and we measure it rather than guess.

## 17, Jon Postel
- Quote: "Be conservative in what you do, be liberal in what you accept from others."
- Source: RFC 793, Transmission Control Protocol, 1981, §2.10, p. 13. https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc793.txt
- Takeaway: A phone number typed with spaces, brackets or +44 is still a phone number. A form should accept it and tidy it up instead of sending it back with an error.

## 18, Hubble
- Story: Hubble's main mirror was ground to the wrong shape because a lens in the test instrument sat 1.3 mm out of place. Two other tests showed the error, and "both indicators of error were discounted at the time as being themselves flawed."
- Source: The Hubble Space Telescope Optical Systems Failure Report, NASA TM-103443, Nov 1990. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19910003124
- Takeaway: When a test fails, the thing under test might be what's wrong. We find out why before we decide the test is broken.

## 19, Jakob Nielsen
- Quote: "Users spend most of their time on other sites."
- Source: "End of Web Design", Nielsen Norman Group, 22 July 2000. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/end-of-web-design/
- Takeaway: People learn how websites work everywhere else. The basket goes top right and the logo goes home, and a small business site isn't the place to be clever about that.

## 20, John Snow
- Story: In 1854 John Snow traced a cholera outbreak in Soho to one water pump on Broad Street. He showed his count of cases to the parish board on a Thursday evening, and "the handle of the pump was removed on the following day."
- Source: On the Mode of Communication of Cholera, 2nd ed. (1855). https://archive.org/details/b28985266
- Takeaway: He didn't rebuild London's water supply. Most site problems need one change in the right place, and the evidence tells you where.

## 21, Ward Cunningham
- Quote: "Shipping first time code is like going into debt."
- Source: "The WyCash Portfolio Management System", OOPSLA '92. https://c2.com/doc/oopsla92.html
- Takeaway: A quick fix is fine if someone comes back to it. Left alone, it costs a little more every time the site changes.

## 22, Dieter Rams
- Quote: "Good design is as little design as possible."
- Source: Ten principles of good design, no. 10, published by Vitsœ. https://www.vitsoe.com/gb/about/good-design
- Takeaway: The aim is a site where nothing gets between the visitor and what they came to do.

## 23, G. K. Chesterton
- Story: Chesterton imagined a reformer who finds a fence across a road and says, "I don't see the use of this; let us clear it away." The wiser answer: if you don't see its use, go and find out before you touch it.
- Source: The Thing (1929), "The Drift from Domesticity". https://archive.org/details/thingwhyiamcatho00ches_0
- Takeaway: Old sites are full of fences: a plugin, a redirect, a form field nobody remembers adding. We find out what each one does before we take it out.

## 24, Blaise Pascal
- Quote: "The present letter is a very long one, simply because I had no leisure to make it shorter."
- Source: Provincial Letters, Letter XVI (1656), tr. Thomas M'Crie. https://archive.org/details/theprovinciallet00pasciala (Often wrongly credited to Twain or Wilde.)
- Takeaway: Short copy takes longer to write than long copy, and that time is part of the job.

## 25, Edsger W. Dijkstra
- Quote: "Program testing can be a very effective way to show the presence of bugs, but is hopelessly inadequate for showing their absence."
- Source: "The Humble Programmer", ACM Turing Lecture, 1972 (EWD340). https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~EWD/transcriptions/EWD03xx/EWD340.html
- Takeaway: A test that passes can still be checking nothing. Every check on our contact form has been watched failing first, so we know it can.

## 26, Louis Sullivan
- Quote: "Form ever follows function, and this is the law."
- Source: "The Tall Office Building Artistically Considered", Lippincott's Monthly Magazine, March 1896.
- Takeaway: A page's layout comes from its job. A contact page is built around the form, and a menu page around the menu.

## 27, Wilbur Wright
- Quote: "If you are looking for perfect safety, you will do well to sit on a fence and watch the birds."
- Source: "Some Aeronautical Experiments", Western Society of Engineers, 18 Sept 1901. https://invention.psychology.msstate.edu/i/Wrights/library/Aeronautical.html
- Takeaway: He went on to say you learn by trying the machine yourself. A site is the same: you learn how it behaves when real people use it.

## 28, Jakob Nielsen
- Quote: "People rarely read Web pages word by word; instead, they scan the page."
- Source: "How Users Read on the Web", Nielsen Norman Group, 1997. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/
- Takeaway: So the important word goes first, paragraphs stay short, and headings say what's underneath them.

## 29, Toyota
- Story: Toyota's production system goes back to Sakichi Toyoda's automatic loom, which stopped itself when a thread broke. Toyota calls the idea jidoka: stop the moment something is wrong, so bad work goes no further.
- Source: Toyota Motor Corporation, "Toyota Production System". https://global.toyota/en/company/vision-and-philosophy/production-system/
- Takeaway: If a contact form can't send, it should say so instead of pretending it worked. Ours alerts us when a send fails.

## 30, Vitruvius
- Quote: "All these must be built with due reference to durability, convenience, and beauty."
- Source: The Ten Books on Architecture, Book I, ch. III, tr. Morris Hicky Morgan (1914).
- Takeaway: Two thousand years on, the brief for a website hasn't changed: it keeps working, it's easy to use, and it looks right.

## 31, Abelson and Sussman
- Quote: "Programs must be written for people to read, and only incidentally for machines to execute."
- Source: Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs, preface to the first edition (1985).
- Takeaway: Someone else may edit your site one day. We build it so they can read it, and you own the logins, so you can let them in.

## 32, Government Digital Service
- Quote: "Making something look simple is easy. Making something simple to use is much harder."
- Source: Government Design Principles, no. 4, GOV.UK, 2012. https://www.gov.uk/guidance/government-design-principles
- Takeaway: A clean look is the easy part. The work is in making a booking, a payment or a form simple to finish.

## 33, Donald Knuth
- Quote: "Premature optimization is the root of all evil."
- Source: "Structured Programming with go to Statements", Computing Surveys 6(4), 1974, p. 268. (Often credited to Hoare.)
- Takeaway: We measure a page before we try to speed it up, then fix whatever makes people wait.

## 34, Steve Souders
- Quote: "80-90% of the end-user response time is spent on the frontend. Start there."
- Source: "The Performance Golden Rule", 10 Feb 2012. https://www.stevesouders.com/blog/2012/02/10/the-performance-golden-rule/
- Takeaway: His own later check found it nearer three quarters, but the point stands. Most of the wait on a slow site is images, fonts and scripts, so that's where we look first.

## 35, McIlroy, Pinson and Tague
- Quote: "Make each program do one thing well."
- Source: "UNIX Time-Sharing System: Foreword", Bell System Technical Journal 57(6), 1978.
- Takeaway: Pages are the same. That's why the home page is short and each project has a page of its own.

## 36, Tony Hoare
- Story: In his 1980 Turing lecture, Tony Hoare said there are two ways to design software: make it so simple that there are obviously no deficiencies, or so complicated that there are no obvious deficiencies. "The first method is far more difficult."
- Source: "The Emperor's Old Clothes", Communications of the ACM 24(2), 1981. https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spr10/cos333/hoare.turing.pdf
- Takeaway: On a simple site you can see what's broken. On a clever one, you find out from a customer.

## 37, Edsger W. Dijkstra
- Quote: "Simplicity is a great virtue but it requires hard work to achieve it and education to appreciate it."
- Source: "On the nature of Computing Science" (EWD896), 1984. https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~EWD/transcriptions/EWD08xx/EWD896.html
- Takeaway: His next line was "complexity sells better." A short site can look like less for the money. It's usually more work to make, and easier to live with.

## 38, Tim Berners-Lee
- Quote: "It is critical that the Web be usable by anyone, regardless of individual capabilities and disabilities."
- Source: W3C press release launching the Web Accessibility Initiative, 7 April 1997. https://www.w3.org/press-releases/1997/wai-launch/
- Takeaway: Anyone includes people with screen readers, shaky hands, old phones and slow signal. Every page we build is checked with axe before it's done.

## 39, Richard Feynman
- Quote: "For a successful technology, reality must take precedence over public relations, for nature cannot be fooled."
- Source: Rogers Commission Report, vol. 2, Appendix F, 1986. https://www.nasa.gov/history/rogersrep/v2appf.htm
- Takeaway: A launch post is public relations. Whether people can use the site shows up later, in the enquiries.

## 40, Kinneir and Calvert
- Story: Jock Kinneir and Margaret Calvert were told "on no account" to draw new lettering for Britain's road signs, because the German one was "perfectly adequate". They drew Transport anyway, built for reading at speed, and it's still on the roads.
- Source: Margaret Calvert, Woman at Work (Thames & Hudson), extract published 12 Feb 2026. https://thamesandhudson.com/blogs/all-news-features/designing-the-transport-typeface-margaret-calvert-on-the-making-of-britain-s-road-signs
- Takeaway: Calvert says Kinneir aimed for "clarity and creating a system that would not date." Clear lasts longer than fashionable.

## 41, Charles Babbage
- Story: Babbage wrote that he'd twice been asked by members of Parliament: "if you put into the machine wrong figures, will the right answers come out?"
- Source: Passages from the Life of a Philosopher (1864). https://archive.org/details/passagesfromlif01babbgoog (Page number not confirmed.)
- Takeaway: A form can't fix a wrong answer it lets in. A good one checks what people type while they type it, not after they press send.
