What changes when the site runs on Cloudflare instead of a usual web host.

Cloudflare bills you directly for anything past its free allowance. We tell you which plan fits before you decide.

A usual web host

  • A server or hosting plan to keep updated
  • Pages sent from one place, wherever the visitor is
  • Forms and email added through plugins

On Cloudflare Workers

  • Cloudflare runs the servers and keeps them updated
  • Pages sent from Cloudflare's network, close to each visitor
  • Forms, spam checks and email written into the site

Why we build on Workers

Speed comes first

Cloudflare runs your site from data centres close to each visitor, so pages start arriving quickly wherever they are. On 27 November 2026, our five sites on Workers began sending a page in 71 to 103 milliseconds from a UK connection.

What does Cloudflare cost me?

Cloudflare's free plan covers up to 100,000 requests a day, which is plenty for most small business sites. If yours needs more, Cloudflare's paid plan starts at $5 a month, and Cloudflare bills you for it directly. Cloudflare doesn't charge for bandwidth.

What if I want to move off it?

Most of the site is ordinary HTML, CSS and JavaScript, which any web host can serve. Parts that use Cloudflare's own services, such as its spam check or its database, would need replacing. We tell you which parts those are before we build.

What if my site outgrows it?

Cloudflare handles far more visitors than a small business site will ever see. What can outgrow a Worker is heavy work: long jobs, a large database or a search engine. When a site needs that, we keep the pages on Cloudflare and run the heavy part on a server in your name. Flintmere runs on its own server for that reason: its catalogue checks are heavy work.

Who looks after it?

Cloudflare keeps the servers running and updated, so there's nothing for you to patch. For changes after launch, every client can take a care plan with a set number of small edits each month. Anything outside the plan is quoted and agreed in writing first.
