Read-only research. No logins, no forms. Quotes are short excerpts; reviewer names left out.

## Is "they kept my domain/logins" the top pain?

No. It comes **6th by frequency** (5–7 of 86). It is the most severe when it happens, because people say they lost the business's address and feel "held over a barrel". It still isn't what most buyers complain about.

**Being ghosted is the biggest pain** (19 primary, about 25 mentions). Close behind is **money that wasn't what they were told**: "free" that isn't, charges with no warning, refunds that never come (20).

Being ghosted and not getting the site (themes 2 and 3) together make up 38%.

How this maps to eazy/access's confirmed promises:
- "a person replies within two working days" answers theme 2.
- "fixed price per stage, nothing extra unless agreed in writing" answers themes 1 and 7.
- "launch date agreed before work starts" answers theme 3.
- "you own the domain, hosting and logins from day one" answers theme 6.

Optiflow's 5-star reviews praise the same thing in reverse: fast replies and being "always available".

Bias: the sample leans towards high-volume "free" or pay-monthly sellers and Yell. Freelancers who charge per project, like eazy/access, are under-represented.

## Part B: competitor ad images (Meta Ad Library, GB, active)

Screenshots are in `competitors/`:
- `adlib-website-design-full.jpg`: "website design", about 3,000 results
- `adlib-web-designer-full.jpg`: "web designer", about 260 results
- `crop-wd-*.jpg`, `crop-wdes-*.jpg`: slices of the two full-page screenshots

Both pages were public and loaded without a login. Media thumbnails were visible, but videos were not played.

What the first page of each search showed:
- **Vertical video dominates**, roughly 70% of the cards. Most are talking-head or UGC selfie clips with a burned-in caption ("unethical but legal ways to…", "Bro made $20,000,000…").
- **Text-heavy price offers.** Storesbybeckett runs dozens of near-identical ads with "£20" in the copy. One static is a solid red block with white text listing first names and earnings. Many lead with a price anchor: "Instead of £1,997 … only £20".
- **Device mockups.** A UK freelancer (mikezak.co.uk) runs a carousel on navy and electric-blue panels: laptop and phone mockups of client sites, question headlines such as "Is Your Website Keeping Up with the Competition?", a headshot badge with a name bar, and an "Agency vs freelancer, Save over 50%" comparison panel.
- **Local-business photo.** One Cornwall studio shows a photo of its own shopfront signage, with "Affordable, Local Web Design" and a "Call now" button.
- **Product-box and badge style** (AI courses): a "SALE ENDS SOON" ribbon, a price sticker, ticked lists.
- **Not seen:** before/after website comparisons and stock photos of smiling business people.
- **Off-topic results:** many ads in the results aren't web design at all (AI courses, dropshipping, Upwork lead generation, newsletters). Very few are from local UK web designers.
- **No Birmingham ads** were seen on the first page of either search.

Implication: a calm, static image showing a real product on a phone, with a single price-free promise, would look different from both the shouty £20 offers and the talking-head videos. The busiest rival look is navy and blue mockups with a founder headshot.
